## 1. Executive Summary

- **The corpus is debug-scale, and this must stay explicit:** SDS-001 contains 563 documents and **250,695 tokens total** (train 230,168 / valid 10,043 / test 10,484). EXP-001 processed **2,457,600 tokens**, i.e. **10.68 epochs** over the train shard — the model saw each training token roughly 10.7 times, not 2.46M unique tokens.
- **The model is data-starved by ~2 orders of magnitude relative to its parameters:** unique tokens / parameters = 250,695 / 1,349,248 = **0.186** (train-only: 0.171). A 1.35M-parameter model with only ~230K unique training tokens across ~10.7 epochs is at high memorization risk, and the observed valid–train loss gap (4.3299 vs 3.9207, **+0.409 nats**; perplexity ~75.9 vs ~50.4) confirms weak generalization.
- **Contamination test PASSED:** the deterministic split (seed 42, 94/4/rest) was re-executed and reproduced the recorded 529/22/12 split exactly. Document-ID overlap, exact-text-hash overlap, and normalized-256-character-prefix overlap between train/valid/test are all **zero**. No duplicate documents survive dedup inside any split.
- **Syrian dialect data is tiny and synthetic:** 117 dialect documents (3 of 120 raw rejected as too short), **13,365 tokens** = **5.81% of the train shard** (13,061 train / 174 valid / 130 test). All of it is template-generated by `syria_ai/data/seed_synthetic.py` — it is not natural dialectal speech.
- **Code data is entirely absent:** all 80 `seed_code.jsonl` snippets were rejected by validation (too short, < 200 chars). The final corpus contains **zero code documents**.
- **License labeling has a material inaccuracy:** `corpus_fetch.py` tags all 392 Wikipedia documents as "CC BY-SA 4.0", but live Wikipedia text is distributed under **CC BY-SA 3.0 with GFDL dual licensing**. Additionally, `ATTRIBUTION.md` lists titles and URLs but **does not list the contributing authors**, does not link the license per entry, and does not declare the modifications (truncation to 6,000 chars, cleaning, PII redaction) — all required attribution elements for any public redistribution or weight release.
- **Language mix of the 250,695 tokens:** Modern Standard Arabic Wikipedia 152,701 (60.9%), English Wikipedia 79,411 (31.7%), synthetic ar/en mixed seeds 5,218 (2.1%), synthetic Syrian-dialect templates 13,365 (5.3%). Wikipedia accounts for 92.6% of all tokens.
- **Note on the project registry:** `docs/DATASET_REGISTRY.md` lists DS-001..DS-008 totalling ~10.8M "training-ready" documents. Those are registry/planned entries. **The corpus actually trained in EXP-001 is SDS-001 with 563 documents / 250,695 tokens.** The two must not be conflated in public communication.

## 5. Unique Training Tokens vs Total Processed Tokens

These two figures were both reported around EXP-001 and must never be conflated:

| Quantity | Definition | Value | Where computed |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Unique training tokens** | Distinct token positions in the packed train shard — the size of the corpus the model's learning updates draw from (one epoch = one pass over this) | **230,168** | `data/processed/shards/train.bin` size / 2 bytes |
| Unique tokens across all splits | Same, summed over train+valid+test | **250,695** | sum of the three shard sizes |
| **Total processed tokens** | Cumulative token presentations to the model during training = steps x batch x block_size = 1,200 x 8 x 256 | **2,457,600** | `training_metadata.json` (`tokens_processed`), arithmetic confirmed in `metrics.json` config (batch_size 8, grad_accum 1, block 256) |

Derived quantities:

- **epochs_over_data = 2,457,600 / 230,168 = 10.68** (about 112.4 steps per epoch at 2,048 tokens/step).
- Total processed / total unique (all splits) = 2,457,600 / 250,695 = **9.80**.
- Tokens-per-parameter: train-only 0.171; all-splits 0.186; processed 1.82.

**Why this matters:** the model did NOT learn 2.46M unique tokens. It repeatedly cycled over the same 230,168 tokens ~10.7 times. "Tokens processed" measures compute and update steps; "unique tokens" measures the information budget. Any communication that cites 2,457,600 as a data-scale figure is overstating the corpus by a factor of ~10.7. The honest data-scale statement is: **"trained on a 563-document, ~231K-token corpus for ~10.7 epochs."**

## 6. Overfitting Risk Analysis

The risk profile is unambiguous: this run sits deep in the memorization regime by construction.

| Indicator | Value | Reading |
|---|---:|---|
| Parameters | 1,349,248 | decoder-only baseline, vocab 4096, block 256, 4 layers, d_model 128, weight tying |
| Unique tokens (train) | 230,168 | train-tokens/params = **0.171** |
| Unique tokens (all splits) | 250,695 | unique-tokens/params = **0.186** |
| Epochs over train data | 10.68 | each token presented ~10.7 times |
| Final train loss | 3.9207 (~ppl 50.44) | still high in absolute terms |
| Final valid loss | 4.3299 (~ppl 75.94) | **gap = +0.409 nats (+51% perplexity)** |
| Best valid loss | 4.3171 (~ppl 74.97) | valid loss did not improve in the last stretch |

Analysis:

1. **Structural risk.** A rule of thumb for healthy LM training is many tokens per parameter (Chinchilla-style target ~20 tokens/param; even compute-starved debug runs usually want several). Here the ratio is 0.17-0.19 unique tokens per parameter, and the optimizer saw the same 230K tokens ten times over: effectively ~1.82 presentations per parameter. With 1.35M trainable parameters chasing 230K tokens of information, the network has ample capacity to memorize the corpus rather than learn transferable language structure.
2. **Observed generalization gap.** Valid loss exceeds train loss by 0.409 nats (4.3299 vs 3.9207), i.e. validation perplexity is ~1.51x train perplexity. The gap is moderate rather than catastrophic, but the direction is clear and, combined with indicator 1, is best read as early memorization plus genuine under-training, not as harmless noise: the validation set (10,043 tokens, 22 docs) is small enough that its loss estimate carries wide variance.
3. **What memorization vs. generalization would look like here.** Generalization: valid loss at or below train loss, stable or falling under longer training, generations that compose novel sentences. Memorization: growing valid-train gap across epochs, generation reproducing verbatim template lines from the synthetic seeds (the training report's samples — e.g., "شو الأثمنة بتعلمنا", "السوق في ... الأثمنة مرتفعة شوي" — are near-verbatim `seed_synthetic.py` template strings, which is consistent with memorizing the 13K-token dialect slice), and near-zero loss on train blocks while valid stays ~4.3.
4. **Honest verdict.** A 1.35M-parameter model trained ~10.7 epochs on ~230K unique tokens is at **high memorization risk**, and valid loss 4.33 (perplexity ~75) confirms weak generalization. This is acceptable for the stated purpose of EXP-001 (pipeline validation), but it must be labeled as such: nothing at this scale demonstrates language modeling quality, and the run provides no evidence about how the architecture behaves at healthy data ratios.
5. **Perplexity provenance.** `docs/TRAINING_REPORT_EXP-001.md` cites perplexity 75.35 from `evaluation_results.json`. That file **does not exist** in `artifacts/experiments/EXP-001/` (only `checkpoint.pt`, `log.jsonl`, `metrics.json`, `training_metadata.json`). The 75.35 figure is consistent with `train_debug.py:evaluate_n` computing held-out perplexity on `valid.bin` with `max_batches=30` (vs the 12-batch in-training protocol that yields e^4.3299 = 75.94). **UNKNOWN — needs regeneration of the evaluation artifact** so the reported number has a persisted source. Importantly, the test shard was not used for any recorded EXP-001 metric, which is correct practice.

## 7. Deduplication Methods Used

Implemented in `syria_ai/data/engine.py:deduplicate`, executed in `prepare_data.py` after cleaning/redaction and before splitting:

1. **Exact dedup:** SHA-256 hash of the whitespace-normalized, lowercased full text (`_norm_key(text)`); first occurrence kept.
2. **Near-dup key:** the same normalized text truncated to its first **256 characters**; documents sharing this prefix are dropped as near-duplicates. This catches shared-prefix boilerplate but not general fuzzy similarity (no minhash/LSH, no shingling, no embedding similarity).

Recorded result on SDS-001 (recomputed, identical): input 563, kept 563, removed_exact 0, removed_near 0, dedup_ratio 0.0. No duplicates existed at the input (all 392 wiki titles unique; synthetic generators draw from large combination spaces, and the three rejected groups explain the size deltas).

Limitations: the 256-char prefix rule is a weak near-dup detector; template-generated dialect docs share vocabulary but not prefixes, so residual template similarity (same sentences reordered across docs) is not counted as duplication. This is acceptable at debug scale but insufficient for any larger corpus build.

## 8. Validation Contamination Test — Method and Result

**Method (exact):** the entire preparation pipeline was re-executed deterministically in read-only mode: `load_jsonl` over the four seed files in the fixed order used by `prepare_data.py`, then `clean_text`, `privacy_filter`, language field pass-through (no document carried `language: "unknown"`), `validate_document(min_chars=200, max_chars=8000)`, `deduplicate`, then `split_dataset(docs, seed=42, train=0.94, valid=0.04)` — which shuffles index list `[0..562]` with `random.Random(42)` and slices 529/22/12.

**Result:**

| Check | train vs valid | train vs test | valid vs test |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Document-ID overlap | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Exact-text (SHA-256 of normalized text) overlap | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Normalized-256-char-prefix overlap | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Intra-split exact dups | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Intra-split prefix dups | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The re-executed split sizes (529/22/12) match `dataset_stats.json` exactly, and re-encoding all three splits with the cached tokenizer reproduced `train.bin`, `valid.bin`, `test.bin` **bit-for-bit** (230,168 / 10,043 / 10,484 tokens). Therefore the shard files provably correspond to a document-level disjoint split: no training document appears in validation or test, and no cross-split duplicate text exists by the two checks applied.

**What was NOT verified (stated explicitly):**

- Semantic near-duplication below the 256-char-prefix threshold (e.g., two wiki articles about the same entity from different categories sharing most body text) was not tested — no fuzzy matching was run.
- Token-level overlap between splits (short template sentences appearing in both train and valid dialect docs) was not measured; with only six templates and fixed word lists, incidental sentence overlap between synthetic docs in different splits is plausible. This is a property of synthetic template data, not of the split logic.
- The tokenizer was trained on the train split only (`prepare_data.py` step 4) — verified by code inspection; no data leakage through the tokenizer was found, though the exact token-frequency state of the tokenizer training corpus was not independently re-derived.

## 10. Wikipedia Licensing Review

**What Wikipedia text actually is.** Live Wikipedia article text is published under a dual license: **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 (CC BY-SA 3.0) and the GNU Free Documentation License (GFDL)**, per the Wikimedia Foundation Terms of Use. The fetch script (`corpus_fetch.py`) labels every fetched document `"license": "CC BY-SA 4.0"` and writes that label into `dataset_stats.json` and `ATTRIBUTION.md`. **This labeling is likely incorrect as to version** and must be verified against the Terms of Use at the retrieval date, then corrected in the dataset metadata. Treating the corpus as "CC BY-SA 4.0" overstates the license grant; the safe assumption is CC BY-SA 3.0 + GFDL dual licensing.

**Attribution requirements (BY clause), as generally understood for CC BY-SA — not legal advice:**

1. **Credit the contributors.** Attribution must be given to the individual article authors. Wikipedia provides this via each article's page history and its "View history"/citation tools. The current `ATTRIBUTION.md` lists **titles and canonical URLs only — it does not list authors and does not link the per-article history**. This is necessary but not sufficient attribution as commonly practiced (Wikipedia's own reuse guidance recommends linking the article or its history so readers can find the author list).
2. **Link the license.** Each redistribution must link the license text. The current header links nothing; the per-entry lines say "CC BY-SA 4.0" without a URL (the URL exists in each JSONL record's `license_url` but is not surfaced in `ATTRIBUTION.md`).
3. **Indicate modifications.** All wiki texts were modified: truncated to at most 6,000 chars, HTML-stripped/whitespace-normalized, and 2 long-number PII patterns redacted. No notice of modification is currently present in `ATTRIBUTION.md`. This must be added.
4. **Keep the attribution reasonable.** A bundled, machine-readable attribution file (JSONL `doc_id -> title, URL, revision/authors pointer, license`) is the appropriate mechanism at this scale and is easy to generate from existing fields — except author lists, which need per-article history retrieval (**UNKNOWN — needs a fetch of revision/author data; not present in the current JSONL records**).

**ShareAlike (SA) implications:**

- Any **redistributed derivative of the corpus** (the cleaned corpus itself, an expanded corpus, a published dataset) must be licensed under the same/compatible ShareAlike license (CC BY-SA 3.0, or a later compatible version where the licensor permits — careful, version forwarding depends on the license text and the Wikimedia terms; do not assume 4.0 without verification).
- **Model weights:** whether training a model on CC BY-SA text produces "adapted material" that must be ShareAlike-licensed is legally unsettled and jurisdiction-dependent; mainstream practice varies, and many projects release weights under their own chosen license with attribution to the corpus. This review does not assert a legal position. What is factually relevant here: with ~10.7 epochs over 230K tokens and a 1.35M-parameter model, **verbatim regurgitation of licensed text in model outputs is a realistic scenario**, and EXP-001's own reported generations already echo corpus/template lines. If outputs reproduce Wikipedia text, those outputs carry the BY/SA obligations of the source.
- **GFDL fallback:** because Wikipedia is dual-licensed, a distributor may generally choose either license for the text (Wikipedia's terms describe the mechanics); GFDL compliance has additional requirements (history section, no invariant sections in Wikipedia's case). The project should keep the dual-license statement rather than flattening it to CC BY-SA 4.0.

**What must ship with any public release (checklist):**

- Corrected license statement: "Text from Wikipedia, dual-licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 and GFDL" with links to both licenses and the Wikimedia terms.
- Per-article attribution: title, canonical URL, and a pointer to the author list (article history link or retrieved revision IDs) — requires one scripted fetch; **UNKNOWN today**.
- Modification notice: truncation (<= 6,000 chars), text cleaning, PII redaction of 2 long-number patterns.
- The `ATTRIBUTION.md` regenerated per article (or one merged file) inside any weight/data release, plus the `Project-Authored` (CC0-equivalent per the project's own claim) notice for the 171 synthetic documents.
- A model card statement that outputs may reproduce CC BY-SA/GFDL-licensed text and how to attribute it.

## 11. Data Quality Constraints and Limitations

1. **Scale:** 563 documents / 250,695 tokens total. This is orders of magnitude below any threshold for language competence; it is a pipeline-validation corpus.
2. **Topical narrowness:** 392 wiki articles drawn from 14 categories (Syria-heavy plus 8 generic ar categories and 6 en categories), truncated to 6,000 chars each. Broad knowledge coverage is absent.
3. **Language-label provenance:** language fields come from the fetcher/seed files; `detect_language()` was never exercised in this build. Fine-grained MSA-vs-dialect discrimination for wiki text is therefore **UNKNOWN** (some ar.wikipedia articles contain dialect quotes; not measured).
4. **Dedup depth:** exact-hash + 256-char-prefix only (Section 7). Acceptable here; insufficient for future large crawls.
5. **Split-vs-content caveats:** document-level disjointness is proven, but token-level template overlap across splits in the synthetic slices is plausible and unmeasured (Section 8).
6. **Validation-set size:** 22 docs / 10,043 tokens; valid loss estimates carry high variance (and in-training evaluation used only 12 batches = 96 block draws with wraparound duplication of a few blocks).
7. **PII filtering:** regex-based (email/phone/long-digit). It caught 2 long-number patterns. No named-entity PII detection, no human review. Adequate for debug, inadequate for publication.
8. **Provenance metadata strengths:** every document carries source, license, license_url, URL, title, retrieval timestamp; `ATTRIBUTION.md` is auto-generated. Weaknesses: no revision IDs, no author lists, license version label suspect (Section 10).
9. **Tokenizer caveat:** trained from scratch on the 529 train docs only (corpus_bytes 782,383; corpus_hash 9c4c1ea6bbfa6219; 3,832 merges; vocab utilization 3,948/4,096). Byte-level fallback guarantees no OOV, but with 230K training tokens many merges are overfit to template vocabulary.
10. **Reported-metrics hygiene:** `evaluation_results.json` referenced by the training report is missing from the artifact directory; perplexity 75.35 lacks a persisted source artifact (**UNKNOWN — needs regeneration**). All other cited numbers (losses, tokens, params, duration) reconcile with `metrics.json`/`log.jsonl`.

## 12. Wikipedia Alone Is NOT Sufficient — Data Needed Next

A "Syrian general or specialized model" cannot be built on ar.wikipedia alone, regardless of scale:

- **Coverage:** Wikipedia (even at full ar-wiki scale, ~1.2M articles) is encyclopedic MSA; it contains essentially no dialectal speech, no conversational data, no local administrative/legal/practical registers, and thin coverage of local news, commerce, and everyday practice.
- **Register:** Syrian dialect (Damascene, Aleppan, coastal, rural variants) lives in speech, social media, drama subtitling, and forums — none present here. The current 13.4K synthetic tokens establish only tokenizer coverage.
- **Recommended next data groups (with license care required for each):**
  1. Large licensed Arabic MSA corpora to reach a healthy tokens-per-parameter ratio (billions of tokens) — e.g., openly licensed web/news/encyclopedia aggregations; verify each aggregator's license (ODC-BY/CC0/CC BY-SA) before ingestion into `ALLOWED_LICENSES`.
  2. Dialectal speech transcripts (Levantine ASR corpora, subtitled drama, community-transcribed audio) — requires transcription rights; public-domain or CC-licensed sources preferred; speaker consent/privacy review mandatory.
  3. Dialectal text (social media under platform-licensed research datasets, forums, local literature where rights permit).
